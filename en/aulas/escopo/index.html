Lesson 7 · 15 min

What you are not: scope, limits and when to call a specialist

The fastest way to lose a client's trust is to promise what you cannot deliver. "I guarantee it's secure," "I'll test whether it can be hacked," "I certify it complies with the LGPD (Brazil's data protection law)": each of those sentences belongs to another professional. Knowing how to say what you are not is part of the service, and it earns more credibility than promising everything.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What an AI adoption professional does and delivers
  2. 2What stays with pentest, legal and incident response
  3. 3Answering a client who asks for a total guarantee
  4. 4The 5-line scope card

Goal: write your 5-line scope card: what you do, what you deliver, what you don't do, when you call a specialist, and how you talk about the certificate.

01 · Your scope line

On one side, what you do; on the other, who does the rest.

Click each side of the line.

"AI Auditor" here means AI risk assessment and triage. It is not a pentest or a certified audit.

02 · How to write your scope

Four rules that prevent the wrong promise.

1. Verbs you can deliver on

Write what you do with verbs that can be checked: map, point out with evidence, propose, teach, follow up.

Avoid "guarantee," "certify," and "bulletproof."

2. What it guarantees and what it doesn't

For each deliverable, say what it guarantees and what it does not. A risk triage guarantees that the risks found are documented with evidence; it does not guarantee that no others exist.

That honesty is what makes the client come back.

Ready-made sentenceI point out the risks I found, with evidence and priority. This does not replace a penetration test or a legal opinion.

3. Who to call

Keep on hand the type of specialist for each case: security (pentest and incidents), legal or the DPO (data protection officer) for LGPD (Brazil's data protection law) and contracts, the company's IT team (system changes).

Referring well is also a deliverable.

4. Written authorization

Agree in writing on what you can access and what you will do. Even if you are only looking.

Never test a system without the owner knowing, even with good intentions.

    04 · Template to copy: scope card

    Five lines. It fits in a proposal, an email, or your presentation.

    Scope card (5 lines)
    SCOPE CARD — <your name>
1. What I do: <e.g., I map where AI is used and triage risks with evidence>
2. What I deliver: <e.g., one-page report + 30-day plan>
3. What I do NOT do: <e.g., penetration testing, incident response, legal opinion, guarantee of total security>
4. When I call a specialist: <e.g., suspected leak → security; LGPD or contract question → legal>
5. My credential: <e.g., INEMA qualification certificate (Professional 2030); not a regulated profession nor an ISO certification>
    Guarantee / no-guarantee sentence
    This deliverable guarantees: <what you control and the client can verify>.
This deliverable does not guarantee: <what depends on other factors or on another professional>.
For that, the path is: <specialist or next step>.
    Simulation: illustrative example

    1. I map where AI is used in the company and triage risks with evidence. 2. One-page report and a 30-day plan. 3. I don't do penetration testing, incident response, or legal opinions, and I don't guarantee total security. 4. Leak or attack → security specialist; LGPD and contracts → legal. 5. INEMA qualification certificate; not a regulated profession.

    • "I'll shield your company": a promise nobody keeps.
    • Calling the certificate a "license": it's a qualification, not a professional registration.
    • Leaving item 4 empty: without knowing who to refer to, you end up doing what you shouldn't.

    05 · Practice: your scope card

    Write it for your real case, not the ideal one.

    E1 · Run the simulated conversation

    5 min

    Go through the conversation with Carlos to the end. If you didn't reach "Clear scope", start over.

    Done when: you reached the "Clear scope" ending.

    E2 · Write the card

    15 min

    Fill in the 5 lines in your own words. In item 4, write the type of specialist and, if you have one, a contact.

    Done when: the card has 5 lines, none uses "guarantee" without the "does not guarantee" next to it, and item 4 has at least two cases.

    E3 · Test it with someone

    10 min

    Show the card to a colleague and ask: "what do you think I do and don't do?"

    Done when: your colleague's answer matches lines 1 and 3 of the card.
