Lesson 1 · 15 min

The 2027 map: what AI does well, where it fails, and what it costs

AI gets hard things right and simple things wrong. Anyone who helps a company adopt AI needs to know, for each task, which side of the line it falls on and what it really costs, counting the time spent checking. This lesson gives you the map and the model to use in your work.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What the "jagged frontier" of AI is
  2. 2Test a task before you trust it
  3. 3Calculate the cost per task, including checking
  4. 4Who's who among the vendors

Goal: sort 3 tasks from your work into does well, does with checking, or should not do, with the cost of each.

01 · The jagged line

Every task falls into one of these three zones. Cost decides whether it's worth it.

Click each zone to see examples from Horizonte Distribuidora.

The line isn't straight: the same AI that writes a contract can get a simple sum wrong. That's why you test task by task.

02 · How to build the map for a task

Four steps. Do it with a real task.

1. Choose

Pick a task that happens many times a month and has a result you can check.

A task that happens twice a year rarely pays off: tuning the AI takes more work than doing it by hand.

HorizontePatrícia, the sales manager, puts together a summary of the week's orders by customer every Monday.

2. Test

Run the task in the AI three times with the same material. Then include an edge case: a missing data point, a number outside the usual range.

Compare the three answers. If they change from one run to the next, you already know you need to check.

Edge caseAn order canceled midweek: did the AI add it to the total or take it out?

3. Classify

Ask yourself: if the AI gets it wrong 1 time in 20, is the impact tolerable? If so, it does the task. If the error is costly but would show up in a check, it does the task and someone checks it.

If the error can harm money, health, a contract, or a person, it only prepares the work.

4. Do the math

Add up the time the task takes today, the time to use the AI, and the time to check. If the total doesn't drop, the AI didn't help.

Before you think about AI, see whether a simple rule, a spreadsheet, or an off-the-shelf system solves it. Use the simplest solution that works.

MathToday 90 min. With AI: 10 min to ask + 20 min to check = 30 min. A gain of 60 min per week.

  • Leaving the check out of the math: the gain disappears when every answer has to be redone.
  • Pasting customer data into the test: use examples without names and without documents.

04 · Who's who in October 2026

The names change every few weeks; the method in this lesson does not. Today the main providers are Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI (ChatGPT and Codex), Google (Gemini), xAI (Grok), and the Chinese labs, which publish open models such as Qwen, from Alibaba.

Prices fall fast. On 09/22/2026, Anthropic launched Opus 5.5, saying it reaches the level of Fable 5.1 at 40% less cost than Opus 5. In August, Alibaba opened up Qwen3.8-27B, which runs even on a laptop. Building got cheap; checking is still up to you.

Stanford's AI Index 2026 report calls this the jagged frontier: models that win hard competitions and still get simple tasks wrong. The same report counts 362 documented AI incidents in 2025, up from 233 the year before.
