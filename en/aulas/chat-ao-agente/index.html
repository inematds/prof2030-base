Lesson 2 · 15 min

From chat to the agent that works on its own

"Agent" has become an advertising word. To help a company, you need to know what each tool really does: it only answers, it uses the company's files, it acts in the world, or it works on its own for hours. Each step up calls for more control. This lesson gives you the ladder and the criteria to classify any tool.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The 4 levels: chat, assistant, agent and autonomous agent
  2. 2The 4 questions that classify any tool
  3. 3What control each level calls for
  4. 4The agents that became products in 2026

Goal: classify 5 AI tools at your company into the 4 levels and say what control each one needs.

01 · The ladder of the 4 levels

Each step does more and calls for more control.

Click each step.

Rule of thumb: if the task involves money, has an action that can't be undone or talks to people outside the company, nothing goes out without a person's OK, at any level.

02 · The 4 questions that classify

Ask the questions in order. The first "yes" from the bottom up gives the level.

1. Does it only answer?

If the tool only answers what you write and doesn't keep instructions or read the company's files, it's a chat.

Example: Juliana, in customer service, uses a free chat to improve the English of a reply.

2. Does it read company things?

If it has fixed instructions or reads the company's documents, spreadsheets and folders, it's at least an assistant.

Write down what it reads. An access nobody chose is the most dangerous one.

HorizonteAn assistant with the price list and the product manual for the sales team.

3. Does it act outside the conversation?

If it sends messages, changes a system, creates orders or touches email, it's an agent.

For each action, note: does it need someone's approval first? Is it logged?

HorizonteThe WhatsApp agent that answers customers and closes orders with discounts.

4. Does it work with no one watching?

If it gets a goal and keeps going on its own, overnight or for hours, it's an autonomous agent.

Ask who shuts it off, how fast, and what the spending cap is.

For each agent or autonomous tool, answer:
- Who shuts it off, and how, within 1 minute?
- What is the spending cap?
- What touches money, can't be undone or talks to people outside the company? Does that wait for a human OK?
Prompt · find out what a tool does
I'm going to describe an AI tool the company uses. Help me classify its level.
Tool: <name and what it's for>
What I know: <where it's installed, what connections it has, who uses it>

Answer:
1. Does it only reply, read files, act outside the conversation, or work on its own?
2. What questions should I ask the vendor or IT to be sure?
Don't invent features of the tool: if you don't know, say so.
Simulation: illustrative example
  • Juliana's free chat: chat. Risk: she pastes customer data into it.
  • Sales assistant with the price table: assistant.
  • Sales WhatsApp agent: agent. Gives discounts and creates orders.
  • Email integration: agent that can send and delete email.
  • Customer service summary via API: assistant, but it sends customer conversations outside the company.
  • Classifying by the product name: "agent" in the name doesn't tell you what it does. Ask what it reads and where it acts.
  • Forgetting what employees use on their own: that's "shadow AI," which IT doesn't know about.
  • Thinking chat has no risk: what gets pasted into it can be personal data.

04 · The agents that became products in 2026

In 2026, the agent that works on its own left the lab. In July 2026, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work, an agent that delivers documents, spreadsheets and slides and asks for approval before sensitive actions, like sending email. In April, Anthropic opened Managed Agents in public beta: agents that run in the cloud and are billed by the hour of session.

In Claude Code, the September update brought background computer use, in beta. The pattern is the same: the tool does more on its own, and your role becomes delegating, reviewing and approving. The names will change; the 4 questions will still work.

05 · Practice: classify 5 tools

Use your own company. If you don't have access, use Horizonte.

S1 · List the tools

10 min

List 5 AI tools in use, including the ones employees use on their own. Ask 2 colleagues what they use.

Done when: the inventory has 5 rows, with who uses each tool.

S2 · Answer the 4 questions

20 min

For each tool, fill in what it reads, where it acts, whether it needs approval and whether it's logged. Where you don't know, write "don't know" and note who to ask.

Done when: all 5 tools are classified into one of the 4 levels, and every "don't know" has the name of who will answer.

S3 · The control for the highest step

10 min

Take the highest-level tool and answer: who shuts it off, what the spending cap is, what waits for a human OK.

Done when: all three answers are written down, with a person's name under "who shuts it off."
