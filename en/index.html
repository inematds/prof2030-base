Professional 2030 · Common Core · 8 lessons

Trusting what AI builds: the basics for anyone who will help companies adopt AI

Building with AI got cheap. Trusting what it builds is still expensive. In eight lessons, you learn what AI does well, build a small app and look for flaws in it, recognize the risks that sink projects, and write a one-pager the board understands.

The lessons

In order: understand, build, find the risk, decide on data and rules, say what you are not, and talk to the board.

  1. Lesson 1 · 15 minThe 2027 map: what AI does well, where it fails, and what it costsAI gets hard things right and simple things wrong. Anyone who helps a company adopt AI needs to know, for each task, which side of the line it falls on and what it really costs, counting the time spent checking. This lesson gives you the map and the model to use in your work.
  2. Lesson 2 · 15 minFrom chat to the agent that works on its own"Agent" has become an advertising word. To help a company, you need to know what each tool really does: it only answers, it uses the company's files, it acts in the world, or it works on its own for hours. Each step up calls for more control. This lesson gives you the ladder and the criteria to classify any tool.
  3. Lesson 3 · 20 minVibe coding from the trust side: build an app and look for the flawsVibe coding means asking for a program in plain language and letting AI write the code. It works: in minutes you get an app that opens and looks finished. The catch is that "it opened" doesn't mean "it's safe." In this lesson you build a one-screen app, safely, and then switch roles: you become the one who looks for the flaws.
  4. Lesson 4 · 20 minThe risks that sink projects: exposed key, injection, too much permission and action without approvalMost AI problems at a company don't come from a sophisticated attack. They come from a key forgotten in the code, an agent that obeys any text, a permission nobody chose and an action nobody approved. In this lesson you learn to recognize all four, and you'll find them at Horizonte Distribuidora.
  5. Lesson 5 · 20 minData, LGPD (Brazil's data protection law) and choosing a vendor: where data is allowed to goEvery time someone pastes a customer conversation into an AI, the data travels: to a server, sometimes outside Brazil, sometimes to train another company's model. LGPD (Brazil's data protection law) applies even when an AI is the one reading. This lesson gives you a simple way to decide where each piece of data can go and with which vendor.
  6. Lesson 6 · 15 minRules with set dates: what applies now and what's comingAI regulation feels like a distant topic, but part of it already applies today and part of it has a date on the calendar. Whoever helps companies doesn't need to be a lawyer; you need to know what applies now, what's coming, who it applies to and when to call legal. This lesson organizes all of that into a timeline you build for your own company.
  7. Lesson 7 · 15 minWhat you are not: scope, limits and when to call a specialistThe fastest way to lose a client's trust is to promise what you cannot deliver. "I guarantee it's secure," "I'll test whether it can be hacked," "I certify it complies with the LGPD (Brazil's data protection law)": each of those sentences belongs to another professional. Knowing how to say what you are not is part of the service, and it earns more credibility than promising everything.
  8. Lesson 8 · 20 minA one-pager for the board + What changedYou found the risks; now someone has to decide. A director doesn't read a ten-page technical report: they read one page in two minutes, if it talks about money, customers and what needs to be decided. This lesson closes the course with the final project, a quick diagnosis of Horizonte, and with the panel of what changed in the market.

Who it's for

  1. 1Any professional who will help a company adopt AI with method and safety, even without knowing how to code.
  2. 2Anyone who wants to follow one of the four Professional 2030 tracks: it is the recommended prerequisite (not required).
  3. 3Anyone who already uses AI at work and wants to know where it goes wrong before trusting it.

What you walk away able to do

  1. 1Say, for a given task, whether AI does it well, does it with checking, or should not do it.
  2. 2Build a one-screen app with AI and find three flaws in it.
  3. 3Recognize an exposed key, prompt injection, too many permissions, and action without approval.
  4. 4Decide where a piece of data can go and build the timeline of the rules.
  5. 5Hand the board a one-page diagnosis.
